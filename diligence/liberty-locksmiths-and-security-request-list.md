## How this list is scaled

This is a five-person, sub-$1M owner-operated shop, not a platform. A 150-line institutional request list will
read as an audit and get no response. The list below is therefore split:

- **Phase 1 (~14 items)** — the minimum needed to put a credible number on the business. Frank should be able to
  pull most of it from his accountant and his QuickBooks file in an afternoon.
- **Phase 2** — confirmatory diligence, post-LOI, organized by workstream.

Items marked **★** are the ones that actually move price or kill the deal at this size. If Frank pushes back on
volume, hold the ★ items and drop the rest.

## Phase 1 — Pre-LOI (valuation set)

Goal: revenue quality, true owner earnings, and whether the business survives Frank's departure.

### Financial
1. ★ Income statements and balance sheets, FY2023 / FY2024 / FY2025 and YTD 2026, monthly if available.
2. ★ Federal business tax returns, 2023–2025 (all pages and schedules).
3. ★ Owner compensation and add-back schedule — salary, distributions, vehicle, phone, insurance, any personal
   expenses run through the business. We need to get to a defensible SDE/EBITDA.
4. ★ Bank statements for all operating accounts, trailing 12 months. *(Reconciling deposits to reported revenue
   is standard for any cash-intensive service business; this is not an accusation, it is how we price.)*
5. Merchant processing statements, trailing 12 months (card volume by month).
6. AR aging as of the most recent month-end.

### Revenue quality
7. ★ Revenue by category for the last two full years — emergency/lockout, residential service, commercial
   service, access control installation, camera/CCTV, safes, hardware sales, and anything else material.
8. ★ Recurring revenue schedule: any monitoring, service, maintenance or inspection agreement that bills on a
   recurring basis — customer, monthly amount, start date, term, renewal terms. If there is none, say so
   explicitly. *(This is the single biggest swing factor in what we can pay.)*
9. ★ Top 20 customers by revenue for each of the last two years, with revenue by year. Flag any property
   management company, municipality, school district, hospital or housing authority.
10. Count of active commercial accounts vs. one-time residential jobs, last 12 months.

### The owner question
11. ★ Who holds the New Jersey locksmith license — Frank personally, or an entity/qualifier? Copy of the license
    and its expiration. **If the license runs through Frank personally, this is the first thing we have to solve,
    and it shapes the whole deal structure.**
12. ★ Frank's own role, honestly described: hours per week, how much billable field work he does, which customer
    relationships are his personally, and whether he intends to stay on post-close and for how long.
13. Employee roster (no names needed at this stage): role, tenure, full/part-time, licensed or certified,
    hourly or salary, and who can run a job without Frank on site.
14. Any prior offers, broker engagements, or LOIs on the business in the last three years.

## Phase 2 — Confirmatory (post-LOI)

### A. Corporate and organizational
- Certificate of formation / incorporation and all amendments.
- Operating agreement or bylaws, and any shareholder, partnership or buy-sell agreements.
- Current cap table / ownership schedule, including any options, warrants or promised equity.
- Certificate of good standing, NJ.
- Trade name / DBA registrations, including "Liberty Locksmiths and Security" and any variants.
- Minute book, consents and resolutions, 2016 to present.
- List of all subsidiaries, affiliates and related entities under common ownership.
- ★ Schedule of all related-party transactions — rent paid to an owner-affiliated entity, loans to/from the
  owner, family members on payroll.

### B. Financial detail
- Trial balance and general ledger detail for the last three years (QuickBooks file or export preferred).
- Monthly P&L by category, trailing 36 months.
- AP aging and a schedule of all trade payables.
- ★ Complete debt schedule — bank loans, SBA loans, equipment financing, vehicle notes, lines of credit,
  merchant cash advances, owner loans. Include payoff amounts and any prepayment penalties.
- Schedule of all personal guarantees given by the owner.
- Fixed asset register with acquisition dates, cost and accumulated depreciation.
- Inventory listing and valuation as of the most recent month-end — key blanks, cylinders, hardware, safes,
  access control components. Flag obsolete or slow-moving stock.
- Capital expenditures, last three years, and anything planned or deferred.
- Any financial statements prepared by an outside accountant (compilation, review or audit) and the
  accountant's contact information.

### C. Tax
- State and local tax returns, 2023–2025 — NJ CBT or gross income tax, sales and use tax.
- ★ Sales tax filings and proof of payment. Confirm treatment of labor vs. materials and of service contracts
  under NJ rules — misapplied sales tax on service work is a recurring exposure in this trade.
- Payroll tax returns — 941s, NJ-927, W-3, state unemployment filings.
- Any notices, audits, assessments, liens or payment plans with the IRS or any state or local authority.
- Confirmation of S-corp or other election status, if applicable.

### D. Customers and contracts
- ★ Copies of every written customer agreement — monitoring, service, maintenance, inspection, master key
  system service, and any municipal or institutional contract.
- Any contract with a change-of-control, assignment-restriction or termination-on-sale clause. **Flag these
  early; they determine whether we need consents at closing.**
- Schedule of open jobs / work in progress with contracted value and percent complete.
- Outstanding quotes and proposals over $5,000.
- Warranty obligations outstanding on prior installations, and historical warranty/callback cost.
- Any customer complaints, disputes, credits or refunds in the last three years.
- Bid or pricing sheets currently in use, and the last general price increase.

### E. Licensing, permits and compliance
- ★ All licenses held by the company and by individuals — NJ locksmith license, and if the company does alarm
  or low-voltage work, the corresponding burglar alarm, fire alarm and/or electrical contractor credentials.
  Include license numbers, holders, expirations and renewal history. *(Confirm the exact NJ categories that
  apply — the Division of Consumer Affairs committee structure covers locksmith and alarm work together, and
  we need to know precisely which the business relies on.)*
- ★ Whether each license is held by the entity or by Frank personally, and what the state requires to transfer
  or re-qualify under new ownership. Get this in writing from counsel if there is any ambiguity.
- Municipal business licenses and registrations, Bayonne and any other municipality worked in.
- ★ Surety bonds — issuer, amount, expiration. NJ locksmith bonding requirements and proof of compliance.
- Background check / fingerprinting records for licensed staff, as required.
- Any disciplinary action, complaint or investigation by a licensing authority, ever.
- OSHA logs and any citations, last three years.
- Manufacturer and distributor authorizations — ★ restricted keyway and high-security dealer agreements
  (Medeco, Mul-T-Lock, ASSA, Schlage Primus or similar), safe dealer authorizations, access control and camera
  manufacturer dealer agreements. **Confirm for each whether it survives a change of control** — these are
  often personal to the dealer and are a real part of what we are buying.

### F. Employees and benefits
- Full employee census: name, role, hire date, comp, classification, license/certification held.
- Employment agreements, offer letters, non-competes, non-solicits and confidentiality agreements.
- ★ Whether any employee other than Frank holds a license the business depends on, and that person's retention risk.
- Independent contractor arrangements and 1099s — and the basis for treating them as contractors.
- Employee handbook and written policies.
- Benefit plans — health, retirement, and any employer contribution history.
- Workers' comp claims history, last five years, and current experience modifier.
- Any employment claims, wage-and-hour disputes, or unemployment contests.
- On-call and overtime practices for emergency/after-hours work, and how that is compensated.

### G. Vendors, equipment and inventory
- Top 20 vendors by spend, last two years, with terms.
- Supply agreements, rebate programs and volume commitments.
- Equipment leases — key machines, programming equipment, diagnostic tools.
- Schedule of all tools and equipment, owned and leased, with condition notes.
- ★ Key machine and programming equipment inventory, including transponder/automotive capability if the
  business does automotive work.

### H. Real estate and vehicles
- Lease for the Bayonne premises — full document, term, renewal options, rent, escalators, assignment and
  change-of-control provisions, and the landlord's identity (confirm whether owner-affiliated).
- Any owned real property, with title and any mortgage.
- ★ Vehicle schedule — year, make, model, VIN, mileage, owned or financed/leased, titled to the company or to
  Frank personally, and condition. Service vans are a material asset in a shop this size and are frequently
  titled personally.
- Vehicle insurance, registrations and DOT status if applicable.

### I. Insurance
- All current policies with declarations pages: general liability, commercial auto, workers' comp, property,
  umbrella, cyber, and ★ any errors & omissions or professional liability specific to locksmith work.
- Five-year loss runs for every line.
- ★ Any claim arising from a burglary, unauthorized entry, failed lock or rekey, or key control failure —
  this is the characteristic liability of the trade and we need the full history.
- Certificates of insurance currently issued to customers, and any customer-imposed coverage requirements.

### J. Legal
- All pending, threatened or resolved litigation, arbitration or mediation, last five years.
- Demand letters, attorney correspondence and settlement agreements.
- Judgments, liens and UCC filings against the company or its assets (we will run our own searches as well).
- Any consumer complaints filed with the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs or the Better Business Bureau.
- Contracts with any non-customer third party — referral, subcontracting, or partnership arrangements.

### K. Systems, data and intangibles
- Software in use — field service/dispatch, accounting, access control management — with license terms and
  whether each is transferable.
- ★ Customer database and job history: where it lives, how complete it is, and whether it exports.
- ★ **Key records and master key system records** — customer master key charts, bitting lists, restricted key
  authorization records, and how they are secured. This is simultaneously one of the most valuable assets in
  the business and one of its largest liabilities. Confirm chain of custody and access controls.
- Domain names, website, social accounts, Google Business Profile ownership, and review profiles.
- Trademarks, registered or common-law, including the "Liberty Locksmiths" name.
- Phone numbers — ★ confirm the main line and any tracked numbers are owned by the business and portable, not
  personal to Frank.
- Marketing: current spend by channel, lead sources, and the share of work coming from Google vs. repeat vs.
  referral.

### L. Transaction-specific
- Any existing agreements with brokers, finders or M&A advisors, and the fee terms.
- Owner's intentions on real estate, vehicles and personal assets — what conveys and what does not.
- Frank's post-close objectives: full exit, transition period, earnout appetite, employment or consulting.
- Any employee who has been told about a potential sale, and the communication plan.
